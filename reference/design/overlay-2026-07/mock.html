Moa overlay — companion + ribbons state: dormant tap a ribbon = copy rail · hold = menu · double-tap = history · drag anything = move the unit

Design mock — reference/design/overlay-2026-07

The overlay is a companion between two ribbons.

This grey text is the fake page underneath. It is here so you can judge the one thing that matters: whether the overlay stays out of the way. In its resting state the companion sits at 34% opacity and the ribbons paint nothing at all — no plate, no border, no shadow. Press and hold a ribbon and it solidifies under your finger, then softens back when you let go.

Press Play turn. Text streams into the top ribbon character by character, the way a real transcript arrives. Watch what happens when the line runs past the right edge: the window does not grow and the text does not wrap. The line slides left so the newest character stays pinned at the right, and the left edge dissolves into a 16px fade. That is the whole sliding-window rule. Nothing in the unit ever changes size.

Then the reply streams into the bottom ribbon while your own words stay readable above the companion. Two streams, one unit, one line each.

Gestures. Every element is a drag handle — grab the companion, grab either ribbon, the whole unit moves together and stays exactly where you put it. A single tap on a ribbon solidifies it and reveals the copy button. A hold opens a four-row menu. A double-tap hands off to History, which is a separate surface: the browser side panel or the Android history screen. The overlay never becomes a scrollback.
