# First exercise: name the quantity

## Goal

Turn a broad technical idea into a claim with a quantity, unit, comparison, and
test. Keep speaking freely for the first half.

## Fifteen minutes

### Speak for 7 minutes

Choose one system you know well. Explain:

1. What outcome should improve?
2. What quantity captures that outcome?
3. What changes that quantity?
4. What evidence would change your mind?

Do not stop to edit yourself. If a word feels vague, keep going.

### Review for 3 minutes

Find one sentence that uses a broad comparison such as better, faster, cheaper,
larger, reliable, or efficient.

Fill this frame:

```text
I expect [intervention] to change [quantity]
from [baseline or range] to [target or range],
measured in [unit or scale],
under [conditions],
because [mechanism].
```

If an index is needed, use:

```text
The index combines [components].
Higher means [direction].
The weights come from [rule or evidence].
We judge it by how well it predicts [outcome].
```

### Re-say for 4 minutes

Explain the idea again. Use the frame once. Return to natural speech after the
claim is clear.

### Record for 1 minute

Save:

- the original vague phrase, redacted if needed;
- the chosen quantity and unit;
- the revised claim;
- a 1-to-5 rating for whether the revision still sounds like Nat.

## Vocabulary drill

Practice these distinctions:

- quantity: what varies;
- dimension: the kind of quantity, such as time, energy, or count;
- unit: how the quantity is measured, such as milliseconds or joules;
- scale: how values map to labels or numbers;
- baseline: the comparison point;
- rate: change or events per unit;
- distribution: how observations spread, not only their average;
- percentile: the value below which a stated share falls;
- proxy: a measurable stand-in for the target;
- index: a stated combination of measures;
- weight: each component's influence on the index;
- validity: whether the measure captures the intended concept;
- predictive power: how well the measure forecasts a named outcome.

Use one cue in the next session: "What varies, and in what unit?"
